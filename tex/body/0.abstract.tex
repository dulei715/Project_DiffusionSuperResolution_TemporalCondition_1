\begin{abstract}
% Time series forecasting is fundamental to data-driven decision-making across finance, healthcare, manufacturing, and transportation, yet existing deep learning approaches remain constrained by fixed-length input requirements, inadequate multi-scale temporal modeling, and suboptimal exploitation of spatial-temporal dependencies. To address these limitations, we present MR-CDM, a novel framework integrating three rigorously designed components: a hierarchical multi-resolution trend decomposition module to capture patterns across distinct scales, an adaptive delay embedding and short time Fourier transform mechanism that maps variable-length series into structured two-dimensional representations while preserving essential dependencies, and a multi-scale conditional diffusion process that leverages contextual information to constrain generative trajectories and mitigate stochastic variance. Comprehensive evaluations on four real-world datasets, including electricity load, traffic flow, weather, and solar energy, demonstrate that MR-CDM achieves consistent and statistically significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines such as CSDI, TimeGrad, Informer, and Autoformer, yielding average reductions of approximately six to ten percent in mean absolute error and six to nine percent in root mean square error. Ablation studies further validate the contribution of each component, with the multi-resolution decomposition alone delivering up to an eleven percent improvement in accuracy, while theoretical analyses establish the model's statistical consistency and confirm its computational feasibility with efficient scaling relative to sequence length. Collectively, these results indicate that MR-CDM provides a principled and effective solution for variable-length, multi-scale time series forecasting.

\end{abstract}
